## 1. Purpose

The Delivery Platform presents intelligence generated by the Daily Intelligence Platform to the user.

It does not generate intelligence. It transforms recommendations, critiques, analyses, insights, and operational context into a coherent, accessible, and reviewable reading experience.

The preferred experience is a personalized newspaper. The platform SHOULD feel more like a publication produced by a well-informed chief of staff than a dashboard or task list.

## 2. Objectives

A successful edition SHOULD:

- inform;
- direct attention;
- challenge assumptions;
- maintain interest and curiosity;
- develop decision quality;
- preserve historical context;
- make feedback effortless;
- remain usable on mobile and desktop.

The Delivery Platform MUST NOT optimize for engagement at the expense of accuracy or intellectual diversity.

## 3. Product Boundaries

The Daily Intelligence Brief is not:

- a task manager;
- an analytics dashboard;
- a general news feed;
- a social network;
- a chat archive;
- a notification inbox.

It MAY link to or integrate with those systems, but its primary object is the edition.

## 4. Delivery Surfaces

The platform SHOULD support multiple delivery surfaces while maintaining one canonical edition.

### 4.1 Web edition

The Web Edition is the canonical edition.

It SHOULD provide:

- complete edition content;
- article expansion and comparison;
- counterpoint and evidence visibility;
- recommendation evaluation;
- archive navigation;
- historical context;
- user feedback;
- export or print functions;
- current edition and delivery status.

The Web Edition is the preferred surface for deep reading, interaction, and historical exploration.

### 4.2 Email edition

The Email Edition SHOULD act as a compact front page.

It SHOULD:

- be convenient to receive and read;
- remain readable on mobile;
- contain a bounded number of high-priority items;
- include a clear link to the complete Web Edition;
- distinguish recommendations, evidence, and opinions;
- avoid duplicating unsupported or sensitive content.

Email delivery MUST NOT be the only way to access an edition.

### 4.3 Push notifications

Push notifications are optional and SHOULD be constrained.

They MAY announce:

- edition delivery;
- a high-severity risk;
- a major newly observed recommendation;
- an outcome or supersession that requires attention.

Push notifications MUST NOT become the primary reading experience.

The notification SHOULD contain enough context to be useful without leaking sensitive content to a lock screen or third-party notification service.

### 4.4 RSS or syndication

RSS or syndication MAY support alternative workflows.

Such feeds SHOULD:

- preserve edition identity;
- include only authorized content;
- link back to the canonical edition;
- avoid embedding sensitive private data into public syndication.

### 4.5 Audio and future surfaces

Audio, voice feedback, interactive visualizations, and special editions MAY be future surfaces.

A future surface MUST consume the canonical edition or an explicitly derived representation. It MUST preserve provenance and distinguish spoken synthesis from source text.

## 5. Deployment Model

The platform SHOULD support self-hosting.

A preferred reference deployment MAY use:

```text
Docker Container
        |
        v
Local Host
        |
        v
Web Application
```

The system SHOULD support safe access through:

- a local network;
- VPN;
- reverse proxy;
- private tunnel;
- explicitly authorized remote access.

The user MUST control:

- network exposure;
- authentication;
- encryption;
- external delivery providers;
- cloud dependencies.

No mandatory public service SHOULD be required for core delivery.

## 6. Web Application and PWA Behavior

The Web Edition SHOULD be responsive and mobile-friendly.

It SHOULD support:

- installable progressive web app behavior where supported;
- fast initial loading;
- keyboard navigation;
- readable contrast;
- offline access to cached editions where practical;
- clear online and offline states;
- preserving a feedback action after a transient network failure.

Native mobile applications SHOULD not be required for core access.

Offline access MUST not expose private editions on a device or browser without the user's applicable security controls.

## 7. Editorial Structure

Every edition SHOULD contain identifiable sections.

Potential sections include:

- Front Page;
- Active Projects;
- Recommendations;
- Technology;
- Strategy;
- Critiques;
- Counterpoint;
- Historical Insights;
- Repository Health;
- Personal Interests.

Sections MAY vary by edition mode and available evidence.

Sections SHOULD remain recognizable across editions so readers can develop a stable reading model.

## 8. Front Page

The Front Page SHOULD answer:

“What matters today and why?”

The primary edition SHOULD be consumable in approximately five to ten minutes.

The Front Page SHOULD prioritize:

- material changes;
- unresolved decisions;
- time-sensitive risks or opportunities;
- recommendations with high importance;
- major counterpoints;
- meaningful historical reversals.

The Front Page SHOULD NOT maximize story count.

## 9. Article Structure

An article or intelligence item SHOULD use a consistent structure.

Recommended fields include:

- stable item ID;
- headline;
- summary;
- why it matters;
- supporting evidence;
- uncertainty;
- affected projects or themes;
- recommendations;
- counterpoint or caveats;
- related topics;
- source and generation provenance.

Articles SHOULD distinguish:

- source quotations;
- user context;
- system inference;
- model-generated interpretation;
- predictions;
- opinions;
- verified outcomes.

The interface SHOULD make uncertainty and evidence availability visible without interrupting the complete reading experience.

## 10. Recommendation Presentation

Recommendations SHOULD be visually and structurally distinct from ordinary articles.

Each recommendation MAY include:

- recommendation ID;
- category;
- confidence;
- importance;
- urgency;
- strategic value;
- suggested outcome;
- why it matters now;
- status;
- supporting evidence;
- evaluation controls;
- lineage or supersession.

Presentation SHOULD follow the [Recommendation Engine Specification](recommendation-engine.md).

The system SHOULD not present an action as approved or implemented when it remains only proposed.

## 11. Counterpoint Presentation

Every edition SHOULD reserve meaningful space for constructive challenge.

Counterpoints MAY present:

- alternative architectural approaches;
- contradictory evidence;
- competing strategies;
- opposing expert interpretations;
- risks to the dominant conclusion.

Counterpoint content SHOULD:

- identify the claim being challenged;
- provide evidence;
- disclose uncertainty and source bias;
- remain relevant;
- avoid adversarial or manufactured controversy.

Counterpoints SHOULD not be hidden as an unexplained ranked article.

## 12. Historical Intelligence

The archive and edition SHOULD be able to surface longitudinal context.

Examples include:

- a recommendation has appeared repeatedly;
- a risk has persisted across weeks;
- a project momentum has declined;
- an assumption has changed;
- an earlier outcome contradicted a forecast;
- a strategic theme is increasing or fading.

Historical claims SHOULD identify:

- time range;
- source evidence;
- comparison basis;
- confidence;
- superseding evidence.

## 13. Feedback Presentation

Feedback is a first-class capability in the Delivery Platform.

The article, story, critique, and recommendation surfaces SHOULD support direct feedback defined in the [Feedback Learning Specification](feedback-learning.md).

Candidate controls include:

- Helpful;
- Not Helpful;
- More Like This;
- Less Like This;
- Implemented;
- Incorrect;
- Incomplete;
- Later;
- Already Completed;
- Hide or suppress.

The default interaction SHOULD require at most one deliberate action.

Optional expanded feedback SHOULD include:

- incorrect assumption;
- missing context;
- poor timing;
- excessive specificity;
- inadequate specificity;
- source concern;
- already completed;
- not relevant;
- harmful or sensitive framing.

Expanded feedback SHOULD remain optional and SHOULD not block the initial judgment.

## 14. Feedback Reliability

Feedback controls SHOULD:

- preserve target identity;
- preserve edition and context identity;
- confirm successful submission;
- permit correction or reversal;
- remain available after archive;
- queue safely during network or local service interruption.

The interface SHOULD avoid false confirmation when feedback storage fails.

Behavioral observations SHOULD remain separate from explicit feedback and SHOULD follow weak-signal rules.

## 15. Archive

Every delivered edition SHOULD be preserved according to retention policy.

Candidate archive views include:

- daily editions;
- weekly reviews;
- monthly reviews;
- quarterly reviews;
- project-specific editions;
- recommendation history;
- technology or theme history.

The archive SHOULD remain locally useful even when external services or model providers are unavailable.

## 16. Archive Navigation

The user SHOULD be able to browse editions and intelligence by:

- date;
- topic;
- project;
- recommendation;
- agent or model;
- technology;
- strategic theme;
- confidence;
- outcome status;
- evaluation status.

Navigation SHOULD identify whether it is searching source evidence, generated intelligence, or feedback history.

## 17. Reading Behavior

The platform MAY collect local behavioral signals such as:

- article opened;
- article expanded;
- article dismissed;
- archive return;
- section visited;
- feedback interaction;
- reading duration.

These signals SHOULD remain secondary to explicit feedback and SHOULD follow weak-signal treatment in the [Feedback Learning Specification](feedback-learning.md).

The system SHOULD not optimize toward dwell time or frequent return alone.

The platform SHOULD not collect:

- third-party analytics;
- hidden tracking;
- cross-site profiling;
- external advertising signals.

## 18. Privacy

The platform follows Local-First principles.

The system MUST:

- keep editions and feedback local unless the user enables a remote channel;
- disclose remote delivery and provider access;
- avoid external analytics;
- support deletion of editions and feedback;
- support export;
- prevent sensitive content from public feeds or notifications;
- separate public and private edition paths.

The user SHOULD be able to:

- disable behavioral telemetry;
- delete edition history;
- revoke remote delivery;
- configure retention;
- correct or erase feedback;
- choose which sections enter email or notifications.

## 19. Accessibility

The Delivery Platform SHOULD support:

- semantic headings and landmarks;
- keyboard navigation;
- visible focus;
- readable contrast;
- text resizing;
- reduced-motion preferences;
- screen-reader-readable evidence and feedback controls;
- meaningful link text;
- non-color-only status and classification;
- caption or transcript support for future audio.

The experience SHOULD remain usable without JavaScript for a readable static edition where practical.

## 20. Performance

The platform SHOULD optimize for fast morning access.

Candidate goals include:

- responsive navigation;
- fast initial edition rendering;
- bounded image and asset size;
- incremental archive loading;
- cached canonical edition data;
- graceful rendering under weak network conditions.

Performance targets are implementation thresholds and SHOULD be measured by real-device and network conditions.

## 21. Failure Modes

### 21.1 No context available

The system SHOULD:

- mark the edition's context basis;
- fall back to preference or historically valid intelligence;
- avoid fabricating active projects;
- provide an explicit sparse-context state.

### 21.2 Recommendation failure

The system SHOULD:

- preserve available insights;
- show an edition with no unsupported recommendation section;
- surface collection or generation failure;
- avoid presenting stale recommendations as newly generated.

### 21.3 Collection failure

The system SHOULD:

- identify affected context sources;
- use a clearly labeled last-known context if configured;
- reduce edition confidence;
- continue with unaffected sections.

### 21.4 Delivery failure

The system SHOULD:

- preserve the edition in the archive;
- retry according to a bounded policy;
- report delivery failure explicitly;
- avoid claiming successful delivery.

Email, push, and other providers SHOULD have isolated retry behavior.

### 21.5 Feedback failure

The system SHOULD:

- keep feedback pending locally where possible;
- avoid duplicate submission;
- provide retry and status;
- preserve target identity and timestamp.

### 21.6 Presentation failure

The system SHOULD:

- fall back to readable text;
- preserve article, evidence, and recommendation structure;
- avoid hiding unsafe or unresolved uncertainty.

## 22. Success Criteria

The Delivery Platform succeeds when:

- the edition is read regularly;
- feedback is consistently available and usable;
- recommendations receive evaluation and outcomes;
- reading feels informative and efficient;
- intelligence remains accessible;
- mobile consumption is effortless;
- historical editions remain useful;
- counterpoints remain visible and constructive;
- delivery failures do not destroy editions.

Candidate metrics include:

- primary edition completion;
- edition return rate;
- explicit feedback rate;
- recommendation evaluation rate;
- average front-page consumption time;
- archive retrieval success;
- counterpoint interaction quality;
- mobile usability;
- delivery reliability;
- feedback preservation under failure.

The platform SHOULD measure value without interpreting all engagement as success.

## 23. Future Capabilities

Future editions MAY include:

- personalized editorial voice;
- audio editions;
- interactive visualizations;
- recommendation follow-up workflows;
- weekly executive summaries;
- voice or multimodal feedback;
- agent-generated special editions;
- configurable section layouts.

Future capabilities MUST preserve:

- canonical edition identity;
- provenance;
- privacy;
- feedback semantics;
- anti-echo-chamber safeguards;
- delivery fallback behavior.

## 24. Governance

Changes to delivery channels, section structure, feedback controls, privacy, or archive behavior MUST:

- identify affected vision requirements;
- document provider and privacy impact;
- preserve canonical edition identity;
- preserve traceability;
- define migration for existing editions;
- include fallback and rollback behavior.

The Delivery Platform MAY evolve its presentation. Its role as the usable, accessible, archival, and feedback-enabled intelligence delivery surface SHOULD remain stable.
