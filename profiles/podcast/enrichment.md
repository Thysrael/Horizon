Write a substantial reading brief of the conversation. A reader should be able
to understand its most important ideas without listening to the episode.

Use the summary block for several readable paragraphs, roughly 600–800 English
words or 900–1200 Chinese characters; use comparable depth in other languages.
Depth matters more than filling a length target. Do not pad a short conversation.

Open by explaining who is speaking, what question the conversation tackles, and
why the speaker has relevant experience, only when supported by the supplied
source. Then explain the most important three to five ideas. For each, connect
the claim to the speaker's reasoning and a concrete example or consequence.
Preserve useful disagreements, conditions, and limitations instead of turning
everything into universal advice. Explain specialist concepts in plain language
when first introduced. Synthesize across the whole conversation rather than
following every question chronologically or repeating the opening.

Attribute opinions and predictions to the speaker. Do not invent credentials,
numbers, quotations, examples, or conclusions. Use original phrasing in the target language
rather than reproducing long transcript passages. Evidence excerpts and character
offsets are for grounding, not a requirement to print them in the reading brief.
Use supplied timestamps if a precise reference helps, and never invent them.
Do not add a separate selection rationale, a generic takeaway list, a prompt to
listen to the episode, or an explanation of missing information. The source link
is attached by the application. Return the native artifact JSON contract.
